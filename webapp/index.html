TremorLens · SPANDAN Live

Every structure vibrates at fixed natural frequencies set by its stiffness. Damage means stiffness loss, and the frequencies drop. TremorLens reads that heartbeat two ways: its computer-vision engine tracks sub-pixel motion in ordinary video, and a phone accelerometer provides the contact ground truth. This page runs both, right here in your browser.

WHAT TREMORLENS DOES

SEE: computer vision. OpenCV tracks high-texture targets in ordinary 30–60 fps video using phase correlation, comparing patches in the frequency domain to track motion to ~1/50-pixel quantization (sub-millimeter on a real bridge). Static background patches are tracked too, so camera shake cancels out.

LISTEN: each tracked point becomes a displacement signal; a Fourier transform turns it into the structure's frequency fingerprint (f₁, higher modes, damping). Verified accuracy: 0.31% frequency error on physics-true synthetic ground truth (Colab-reproducible), with the phone-accelerometer channel agreeing to 0.1%.
